## Who owns error recovery and incremental boundaries?

**Split by what kind of fact it is. Error recovery → ch5. Incremental
boundaries → ch6.**

Why ch5 for recovery: nearly everything in §5.13 is a fact about *Perl's
grammar* — that `perly.y` has one `error` production, that `CHECK()` and
`defer` are legal bareword calls and so need escape hatches from any
"cannot start an expression" set, that `sub`/`package` at column 0 are the
strongest anchors in real files, that the `catch`-without-parens and
slurpy-with-default productions exist to give good messages. These cite
`perly.y` and belong next to the productions they cite. What ch6 §6.8 adds
that is *not* grammar — `KindMissing` vs `KindError`, the precomputed
`HasError` flag, the 40-byte cooldown and 20-diagnostic cap, PSC gating —
stays in ch6 under a narrower heading ("tree guarantees and diagnostic
policy for a live buffer"). ch6 §6.8.3's `isSyncToken` and `recover` go;
they are a lossy copy of §5.13.4.

Why ch6 for incremental: a "safe boundary" is meaningless without the
checkpoint, resync loop, splice, and splice-equals-fresh-parse fuzzer that
consume it, and all of those are in ch6. ch6 §6.1.2 already holds the
numbered trigger table that its own triage code (§6.5.1) matches against;
ch5 §5.14.3 is that table paraphrased. ch5 keeps only §5.14.2's table — the
language-side answer to "which Perl constructs can be re-parsed in
isolation?" — because that is what a grammar chapter can say with authority.
ch6 §6.5.2 should cite it by section number instead of naming a predicate
that does not exist.

The test for whether this split is right: after the edit, ch5 should read
correctly to someone building a batch parser with good diagnostics and no
LSP, and ch6 should read correctly to someone who already has ch5's parser
and wants it incremental. Today neither is true, because each chapter
contains half of the other's job.

## Summary

- Lines to cut: **~650-700 of 3204** (≈21%); the chapter lands near the 2,595
  README.md already claims.
- Top finding: **§5.11's `{` heuristic is wrong** — perl says `{}` is a hash
  and `{ $x => 1 }` is a block; ch3 §3.3 is right; delete and cross-ref.
- Second: **§5.14 is a second incremental architecture** competing with ch6,
  plus ch6 points at a ch5 predicate that does not exist.
- Third: **§5.15 re-tells the chapter** (seven topics already stated inline)
  and endorses `isHashLiteral` against A1.1.7 and ch3.
- Also: §5.10 specifies `format` in full against ch1 §1.5's non-goal; §5.1.2
  contradicts ch2 on `__DATA__` placement (ch2 is right, measured).
- Ownership: **error recovery → ch5** (grammar facts, `perly.y`-cited);
  **incremental boundaries → ch6** (machine facts; it already owns the
  trigger table). ch6 §6.8.3's sync list and loop should go.
- Verdict: **fundamentally sound, no restructuring.** §5.2-5.9 (~1900 lines)
  is tight, cited, and verified; the bloat is confined to §5.10, §5.13-5.15,
  §5.17 and PerlOnJava runtime trivia in §5.7. Surgical cuts, one factual fix,
  one ownership handoff.
