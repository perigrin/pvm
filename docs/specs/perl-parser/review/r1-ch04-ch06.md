## The three things I would actually do

1. **Make one chapter own each shared artifact and delete the other copies.** `LexState` is defined three ways (ch2/ch3/ch6, no superset); error recovery is defined twice (ch5 §5.13 better than ch6 §6.8.3); the invalidation trigger list is in four places; the splice-equals-fresh property is in two; perl-lsp's incremental story is told three ways with different numbers. An implementer reading three non-matching `LexState` structs will write a fourth. ~150 lines across ch6 alone, more in ch5/ch7/ch3.
2. **Fix the three statements that are false and the one contradiction.** ch4: 32 levels not 33; named unaries *do* stack; `use strict` does not change the indirect-object parse (the `indirect` feature does — and ch4 never mentions it). ch6: lines 620-625 and 1711-1715 still say PSC is the constraint; ch5 §5.14.5 forbids statement-level splicing while ch6 §6.5.2 starts there. These cost nothing to fix and each one will send someone down the wrong path.
3. **Replace hand-rolled Go with the stdlib call, and drop the per-token `States` array.** `slices.Replace` for `ApplyEdit`, `textproto.ReadMIMEHeader` for framing, plain `Reset` on Go 1.23+ timers, `slices.BinarySearchFunc` in the resync loop instead of a quadratic scan plus a cap. Then delete `States []LexState` — it is 44% of per-document memory, the chapter already names its replacement, and the resync loop only needs it because it resyncs at arbitrary tokens instead of at the safe offsets §6.3.1 already defines.
