The four blocking findings share one root cause. The chain assumes that once the reduce side lands (d199, 3262), the default recognizer refuses precedence-forbidden input. It refuses only part of it. A nonassoc or left-assoc chain like `$a <=> $b <=> $c;` or `1 .. 2 .. 3;` keeps its right-nested derivation until the shift side lands (3282). And an indirob death like `print $fh [];` already happens at d199. A number of existing tests rest on the default recognizer accepting these inputs, and the chain names only some of them. The result is intermediate states that cannot close with `make test` green, plus one differential that becomes vacuous without anything noticing.
